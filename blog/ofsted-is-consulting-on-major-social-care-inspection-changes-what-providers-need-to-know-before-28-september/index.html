Ofsted Is Consulting on Major Social Care Inspection Changes — What Providers Need to Know Before 28 September

Ofsted’s consultation on social care inspection reform closes on 28 September 2026. We explain the proposed five-grade scale, report cards and SCCIF changes.

7 September 2026

Ofsted launched a consultation on 7 July 2026 proposing significant changes to how it inspects children’s social care. The consultation runs until 28 September 2026 and includes proposals affecting the Social Care Common Inspection Framework used for Supported Accommodation, children’s homes and other regulated services.

The proposals are not yet the inspection framework. That distinction matters. Providers should continue to work against the current regulations and current SCCIF unless Ofsted formally introduces a revised framework.

What is Ofsted proposing?

Ofsted has proposed removing the “overall experiences and progress” judgement for services inspected under the SCCIF. It is also proposing a five-point grading scale and report cards for both SCCIF and local-authority children’s services inspections.

The stated aim is to provide a more nuanced picture of quality and align inspection more closely with wider children’s social care reforms, including family networks, enduring relationships and the experiences of children and families.

Why Supported Accommodation providers should care

Supported Accommodation currently receives one of three overall outcomes: consistently strong service delivery, inconsistent quality of service delivery, or serious or widespread weaknesses. A future five-grade model would therefore represent a meaningful change in how inspection outcomes are communicated.

Report cards could also change how commissioners, placing authorities, families and other stakeholders interpret inspection findings. Until Ofsted publishes the final approach, however, providers should avoid redesigning internal quality systems around proposals that may still change.

The consultation also signals the direction of travel

Even before final decisions are made, the consultation is useful because it shows where Ofsted’s attention is moving. It includes stronger emphasis on children’s lived experiences, enduring relationships and how inspection can respond to wider challenges across children’s social care.

What providers should do before 28 September

  • Read the consultation rather than relying only on headlines.
  • Discuss the proposed changes with responsible individuals, registered service managers and quality leads.
  • Consider responding to the consultation if your organisation has practical experience relevant to the proposals.
  • Continue using the current SCCIF and regulations for inspection-readiness work.
  • Watch for Ofsted’s consultation outcome and implementation timetable before changing systems.

SAPC will continue to monitor the consultation outcome. Once Ofsted confirms the final framework, providers will need a clear comparison between the current model and whatever replaces it.
