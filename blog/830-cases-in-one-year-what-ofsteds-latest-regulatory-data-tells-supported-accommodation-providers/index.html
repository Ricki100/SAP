830 Cases in One Year: What Ofsted’s Latest Regulatory Data Tells Supported Accommodation Providers

Ofsted published new Supported Accommodation regulatory activity data on 18 August 2026. We explain the 830 cases, compliance notices and enforcement trends.

7 September 2026

On 18 August 2026, Ofsted published its latest regulatory activity figures for children’s homes and Supported Accommodation. For the Supported Accommodation sector, the data provides one of the clearest pictures yet of how actively the new regulatory system is being used.

Between 1 April 2025 and 31 March 2026, Ofsted investigated 830 cases concerning 400 Supported Accommodation providers that held registered status during the reporting year. One or more cases were logged against 32% of providers that were active during the period.

What do these “cases” represent?

A regulatory case does not automatically mean that a provider was found to be unsafe or non-compliant. Ofsted receives and assesses information from a range of sources, and different cases can lead to different responses. Some are monitored, some become lines of enquiry for inspection, some result in a visit, and some progress to formal enforcement.

Of the 830 cases, 700 had one or more actions recorded by the end of March 2026. The most common response was continued monitoring by the allocated inspector. Other actions included creating a key line of enquiry for the next inspection, bringing inspections forward, monitoring visits, referrals and provider-led investigations.

The enforcement figures are particularly important

During the reporting year, Ofsted recorded 21 restrictions of accommodation, 10 suspensions and 2 notices of cancellation involving Supported Accommodation. It also issued 170 compliance notices to 56 providers.

A compliance notice is a formal enforcement tool. It is different from a recommendation and should not be treated as routine administrative feedback. Providers need to understand the reason for the breach, take action within the required timescale and ensure the underlying weakness has been addressed.

Safeguarding remains central

Ofsted also recorded 210 child-protection concerns relating to 120 Supported Accommodation providers during the same period. This reinforces the importance of effective safeguarding systems, appropriate escalation, accurate notifications and evidence that leaders respond to concerns.

What should leaders take from the data?

  • Do not assume that regulatory scrutiny begins and ends with the full inspection.
  • Review how incidents, complaints, safeguarding concerns and notifications are escalated internally.
  • Make sure managers can demonstrate oversight, not just that individual staff completed a form.
  • Track requirements and recommendations through to evidence of sustained improvement.
  • Use internal audits and mock inspections to find weaknesses before they become regulatory findings.

The data shows a sector moving into a more mature phase of regulation. As the number of providers grows, Ofsted is receiving more information, undertaking more regulatory activity and using a range of tools to address concerns. Providers that build compliance into normal management practice will be better placed to respond.
