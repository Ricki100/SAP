Supported Accommodation Grew 37% in One Year — What That Means for Providers

Ofsted’s 30 July 2026 statistics show Supported Accommodation providers grew 37% in one year. Here is what the growth means for the sector.

7 September 2026

Ofsted published Children’s Social Care in England 2026 on 30 July 2026, and the Supported Accommodation figures show just how quickly the sector is developing.

As at 31 March 2026, England had 1,220 active or suspended Supported Accommodation providers. That represented a 37% increase compared with 31 March 2025. During the inspection year, an additional 330 Supported Accommodation providers were registered.

A much larger market than a year ago

Those 1,220 providers operated approximately 9,350 open or pending premises and offered around 24,700 places. London accounted for the largest share of providers, while the North West had the largest share of premises.

The market is also structurally diverse. Supported Accommodation can include self-contained units, group living restricted to looked-after children and care leavers, group living in mixed settings, and supported lodgings in private residences.

Growth creates opportunity — and pressure

A 37% annual increase suggests continued demand and continued interest from organisations entering the sector. But growth does not automatically translate into sustainable provision. More providers means commissioners and local authorities have more options, while Ofsted has a larger sector to regulate.

For new entrants, registration should therefore be viewed as the beginning of the operational challenge rather than the end. Providers need to demonstrate that their model meets local need, that staff understand the Support Standard and other regulatory requirements, and that quality remains consistent across premises.

Scaling without losing quality

Rapid organisational growth can expose weaknesses that are less visible in a small service. Records may become inconsistent. Management oversight can become stretched. Training may differ between locations. Support planning can become formulaic rather than individualised.

What providers should focus on

  • Commissioning need: understand what local authorities actually require, rather than expanding simply because property is available.
  • Consistency: use clear systems for support planning, safeguarding, recording and management oversight across all premises.
  • Workforce: ensure staff competence keeps pace with organisational growth.
  • Evidence: be able to demonstrate quality through children’s experiences and progress, not only policies.
  • Governance: monitor quality data and identify emerging weaknesses early.

The 30 July 2026 statistics confirm that Supported Accommodation is no longer a small emerging corner of children’s social care. It is a substantial and fast-growing regulated sector. The providers most likely to succeed long term will be those that combine growth with demonstrable quality.
