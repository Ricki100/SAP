Regional Care Cooperatives Are Expanding: What Could This Mean for Supported Accommodation Providers?

DfE published new Regional Care Cooperative guidance on 5 August 2026. Here is what the expanding regional commissioning model could mean for providers.

7 September 2026

On 5 August 2026, the Department for Education published guidance explaining the rollout and responsibilities of Regional Care Cooperatives, or RCCs. The development matters to providers because RCCs are intended to bring multiple public-sector partners together to plan and commission homes and support for looked-after children.

The Department for Education says RCCs bring together local authorities, health services, youth justice and other partners. Five new RCCs had been announced on 8 July 2026, following an expression-of-interest process launched earlier in the year.

Why is government moving towards regional commissioning?

Children’s social care has long faced challenges around placement sufficiency: having the right type of provision, in the right places, for children with different needs. Simply increasing the total number of beds does not solve a mismatch between local demand and available provision.

Regional planning is intended to give participating authorities a wider view of need and supply. The model creates the potential for more coordinated commissioning, shared information and joint approaches to gaps in provision.

What could this mean for Supported Accommodation providers?

The 5 August guidance is not a new set of Supported Accommodation regulations. However, it signals a change in the environment in which providers may be commissioned. Providers could increasingly encounter regional frameworks, shared commissioning plans or procurement activity covering multiple local authorities.

That may reward providers who can clearly demonstrate where their service fits: which young people they are designed to support, what outcomes they deliver, where their premises are located and how quality is managed across multiple local-authority relationships.

Providers should pay attention to demand, not just property

One of the risks in a fast-growing Supported Accommodation market is expanding on the basis of available property rather than identified commissioning need. Regional Care Cooperatives strengthen the case for a more evidence-led approach.

  • Track which Regional Care Cooperative arrangements involve the local authorities you work with.
  • Watch for regional commissioning frameworks, tenders and market-position statements.
  • Be clear about the cohort your service is equipped to support and the evidence behind that claim.
  • Make sure your pricing, staffing model and quality assurance can stand up to more structured commissioning scrutiny.
  • Build relationships with commissioners before opportunities are published, where appropriate.

For Supported Accommodation providers, the key message is not that commissioning has changed everywhere overnight. It is that the direction of travel is becoming more regional, more coordinated and more data-led. Providers that understand this shift early will be better placed to adapt.
