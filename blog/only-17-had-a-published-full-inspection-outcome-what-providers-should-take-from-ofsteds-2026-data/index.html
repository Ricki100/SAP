Only 17% Had a Published Full Inspection Outcome: What Providers Should Take From Ofsted’s 2026 Data

Ofsted data published on 30 July 2026 shows only 17% of Supported Accommodation providers had a published full inspection outcome as at 31 March 2026.

7 September 2026

Ofsted’s Children’s Social Care in England 2026 release, published on 30 July 2026, contains an important figure for Supported Accommodation providers: as at 31 March 2026, only 210 of the 1,220 active or suspended providers had a published full inspection outcome.

That is 17% of the sector. It means many providers were still operating without a published full inspection outcome at the end of the inspection year, underlining how new the inspection regime remains.

What were the outcomes among inspected providers?

Supported Accommodation uses three overall outcomes. Of providers with a published full inspection outcome as at 31 March 2026, 45% had consistently strong service delivery, 48% had inconsistent quality of service delivery, and 7% had serious or widespread weaknesses.

The 48% figure is particularly useful for leadership teams because it highlights the challenge of consistency. A service does not need to be failing everywhere to receive an outcome reflecting inconsistent quality. Variability between premises, staff practice, support plans or management oversight can matter.

Why first-inspection preparation matters

When a provider has not yet had a full inspection, it can be easy to treat the inspection as a future event. In reality, the evidence that will shape that inspection is being created every day through support records, safeguarding responses, young people’s experiences, staff supervision, incidents, complaints and management monitoring.

A rushed pre-inspection exercise cannot recreate months of consistent practice. The strongest preparation is therefore continuous: leaders know where evidence sits, review quality regularly and address weaknesses while there is time to embed improvement.

What does “consistently strong” require?

Ofsted’s current Supported Accommodation framework focuses on the overall experiences and progress of children, how well children are helped and protected, and the effectiveness of leaders and managers. Providers should therefore avoid reducing inspection readiness to document completion alone.

Five questions to ask before the first full inspection

  • Can you evidence how young people are involved in their own support planning?
  • Do risk assessments and support plans reflect current needs rather than historic information?
  • Can leaders demonstrate oversight of incidents, safeguarding and staff practice?
  • Are staff able to explain how their work supports the Quality Standards?
  • Could the organisation produce requested information quickly without a last-minute search?

The 30 July 2026 data shows a sector still progressing through its first cycle of full inspections. For providers yet to receive a published outcome, that creates an opportunity: learn from the data now and build inspection readiness before the call comes.
