Ofsted Outcome 2 in Supported Accommodation: What It Means and What to Do Next

By Taps Kundoro, Supported Accommodation Providers Consultancy · Last reviewed 22 October 2026

An Ofsted Outcome 2 in supported accommodation means inspectors found that "inconsistent quality of service delivery adversely affects some children's experiences." It is not a finding that the service is failing, but it is a clear signal: practice varies too much, and Ofsted expects leaders and managers to put it right. Your next inspection will usually come within about 18 months, rather than the three years a service with Outcome 1 can expect.

This guide explains what Outcome 2 means, how to read your report, and a practical 90-day plan for moving to Outcome 1. Ofsted sets out the outcomes in the Ofsted SCCIF: supported accommodation. For how inspectors work on the day, see what Ofsted looks for in a supported accommodation inspection.

The three supported accommodation inspection outcomes

Supported accommodation is not graded Outstanding, Good, Requires improvement or Inadequate like a children's home. Ofsted uses a three-outcome model instead:

OutcomeWhat Ofsted saysNext inspection
Outcome 1Consistently strong service delivery leads to typically positive experiences and progress for children.Within about 3 years
Outcome 2Inconsistent quality of service delivery adversely affects some children's experiences. Leaders and managers must make improvements.Within about 18 months
Outcome 3Serious or widespread weaknesses lead to significant concerns about children's experiences and progress. Leaders must take urgent action.Within about 6 months

What Outcome 2 usually looks like in practice

Outcome 2 rarely comes from one big failure. It usually comes from inconsistency: strong work with some young people or in some properties, but not everywhere. Typical themes include:

  • Support plans and risk assessments that are not updated after incidents such as missing episodes or exploitation concerns.
  • A six-monthly quality of support review that is late, misses young people's views, or has no clear actions.
  • Notifications to Ofsted or placing authorities that are late or missing. Our guide to supported accommodation notifications to Ofsted covers what must be reported.
  • Gaps in staff supervision, training or recruitment records.
  • Leaders who cannot show they spot patterns across the service and act on them.

Inspectors judge three areas: the overall experiences and progress of young people, how well they are helped and protected, and the effectiveness of leaders and managers. Your report will show which areas held the service back.

Requirements and recommendations in your report

  • Requirements are made when a regulation has been breached. Each one names the regulation and gives a date for compliance. If a requirement is not met, Ofsted will consider enforcement action, such as a compliance notice.
  • Recommendations are about improving practice and refer to the DfE Guide to the regulations. If they are not acted on, Ofsted may turn them into requirements at the next inspection.

Ofsted sends the draft report within 18 working days of the inspection, and you have 5 working days to comment on factual accuracy. Use that window to correct factual errors with evidence, not to argue about the outcome. The final report is published on Ofsted's website shortly afterwards.

Your Outcome 2 action plan: the first 90 days

Days 1 to 14: understand and own it

  • Share the report with your board, managers and staff, and explain what it means for them.
  • List every requirement and recommendation. Give each one a single named owner and a date, and meet requirement deadlines first.
  • Tell placing authorities how you are responding. Commissioners read Ofsted reports, and a clear plan builds confidence.

Days 15 to 60: fix the system, not just the file

  • Audit every young person's file. Our case file audits for supported accommodation show you exactly where records fall short.
  • Bring support plans and risk assessments up to date, and set a rule for updating them after every incident. See our risk assessment and support plan reviews.
  • Check your policies against the regulations using our guide to supported accommodation policies and procedures.
  • Put a supervision schedule in place and check training is current for every member of staff.

Days 61 to 90: prove it is working

  • Arrange independent monthly monitoring visits for supported accommodation to test whether changes have reached every property.
  • Complete a quality of support review that includes young people's own feedback, and track its actions.
  • Book a supported accommodation mock inspection well before the next inspection is due.

How to move from Outcome 2 to Outcome 1

The key word in Outcome 1 is "consistently". Inspectors will test whether improvements have reached every young person and every property, not just the files they saw last time. Keep dated evidence of what changed and why: audits, supervision notes, meeting minutes, and examples of young people's progress.

If you would like an experienced team alongside you, our post-Ofsted inspection support helps providers build a credible plan and evidence the impact. You can read other providers' published reports on Ofsted's Ofsted inspection reports site.

In short: an Ofsted Outcome 2 in supported accommodation is a warning, not a verdict. Act quickly, fix the systems behind the inconsistency, and you can show inspectors a consistently strong service at your next inspection.
