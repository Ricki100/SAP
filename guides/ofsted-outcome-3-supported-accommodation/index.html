Ofsted Outcome 3 in Supported Accommodation: Urgent Next Steps for Providers

By Taps Kundoro, Supported Accommodation Providers Consultancy · Last reviewed 22 October 2026

An Ofsted Outcome 3 in supported accommodation is the most serious inspection outcome. Inspectors found "serious or widespread weaknesses" that "lead to significant concerns about the experiences and progress of children", and leaders and managers must take urgent action. You should expect much closer scrutiny: a further inspection usually within about 6 months, and possibly monitoring visits in the meantime.

This guide explains what happens after an Outcome 3, what to do in the first week, and how to build an action plan inspectors will believe. The process is set out in Ofsted's Ofsted SCCIF: supported accommodation.

What happens after an Outcome 3

  • Case review: Ofsted holds a post-inspection debrief and then a case review no later than 5 working days after the inspection. This decides the next steps, such as when to inspect again or whether to take compliance action.
  • Monitoring visits: if the concerns are serious, Ofsted is likely to carry out monitoring visits. These are usually unannounced, and their frequency is set case by case. They can be as often as weekly.
  • Earlier inspection: Ofsted can inspect again before the 6-month point if significant new concerns arise.
  • Compliance notices: Ofsted can serve a compliance notice when a requirement has not been met, or when it is the best way to protect young people.
  • Further enforcement: Ofsted's social care enforcement policy sets out its wider options. These range from imposing conditions on registration and restricting new admissions to suspension and, in the most serious cases, cancellation of registration.

The first seven days: safeguarding first

  1. Check every young person is safe today. Review risk assessments and support plans, starting with anyone the inspectors raised concerns about, and anyone at risk of going missing or being exploited.
  2. Make any outstanding notifications. If a serious event was not reported, report it now and confirm it in writing. Our guide to supported accommodation notifications to Ofsted explains what is required.
  3. Meet requirement deadlines. Some deadlines may fall before the final report is published.
  4. Talk to placing authorities. Share the findings and your plan openly. Commissioners will see the published report, and hearing it from you first matters.
  5. Brief your staff honestly. Explain what was found, what will change, and what you need from them.
  6. Check the draft report. You have 5 working days to point out factual errors, with evidence.
  7. Decide whether leadership capacity is enough. An Outcome 3 often points to leadership problems. Our Nominated Individual and Registered Service Manager mentoring gives managers experienced support quickly.

Building an action plan Ofsted will believe

Inspectors want to see that leaders understand why the weaknesses happened, not just what they were. A credible plan:

  • Finds the root cause behind each requirement, not just the symptom.
  • Gives every action one named owner and a realistic date.
  • Says how you will know each action has worked, for example a dated audit or feedback from young people.
  • Is reviewed weekly by senior leaders, with independent oversight such as monthly monitoring visits for supported accommodation.

Preparing for monitoring visits and re-inspection

Monitoring visits are short, focused and usually unannounced, so treat every week as inspection week:

  • Keep an evidence folder for each requirement, updated as actions are completed.
  • Make sure staff can explain what has changed and why.
  • Collect young people's own views on whether things feel different.
  • Book an independent supported accommodation mock inspection before the re-inspection, so the real thing is not the first test.

Can a service recover from Outcome 3?

Yes. Providers do move from Outcome 3 to Outcome 1 when leaders act quickly, are honest about what went wrong, and can evidence real change for young people. Our post-Ofsted inspection support is built for exactly this situation. If your report is closer to Outcome 2, read Ofsted Outcome 2 in supported accommodation.

In short: an Ofsted Outcome 3 in supported accommodation demands urgent action. Put young people's safety first, act on every requirement, and build an evidence-led plan, so the next visit shows a service that has changed.
