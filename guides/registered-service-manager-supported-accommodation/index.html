The Registered Service Manager in Supported Accommodation: Role and Requirements

By Taps Kundoro, Supported Accommodation Providers Consultancy · Last reviewed 22 October 2026

Every Ofsted-registered service needs a registered service manager for supported accommodation. They lead the service day to day for regulatory purposes, and Ofsted assesses them before registering them. This guide covers who can do the job, what it involves, and what inspectors look for.

Who can be a registered service manager? (Regulation 12)

Under Regulation 12, a person can only be registered as the manager if:

  • They are of integrity and good character.
  • They have the skills to manage the service effectively, taking into account the number, location and category of its premises, the statement of purpose, and the number of young people.
  • They have at least two years' experience in a relevant position in residential support of children or adults, within the five years before Ofsted receives the application.
  • They are mentally and physically fit to manage the service.
  • Full and satisfactory information is available about them, as listed in Schedule 1 of the Regulations.

The Regulations do not name a specific qualification. Many providers and commissioners look for a recognised leadership and management qualification as evidence of competence, but Ofsted's focus is whether the manager has the skills and experience the service needs.

What the role involves

The DfE Guide to supported accommodation regulations including quality standards explains that the registered service manager manages the whole service, not individual properties. In a small provider the role may be hands-on day to day; in a larger organisation it may be more strategic, supported by a management structure that gives effective oversight. In practice, the role usually includes:

  • Overseeing support plans and risk assessments for every young person.
  • Making sure serious events are notified to Ofsted and others without delay. See our guide to supported accommodation notifications to Ofsted.
  • Speaking to young people after any restraint and signing the record within 48 hours.
  • Staff supervision, training and safer recruitment.
  • Completing the quality of support review at least every six months (Regulation 32) and sending the report to Ofsted within 28 days.
  • Keeping policies current and followed in practice. See supported accommodation policies and procedures.
  • Working with placing authorities, independent reviewing officers and personal advisers.

Registered service manager or nominated individual?

RoleMain responsibility
Registered providerThe organisation, partnership or individual registered with Ofsted, which holds most of the legal duties.
Nominated individualRepresents the organisation and is Ofsted's main point of contact. Supervises how the service is managed.
Registered service managerManages the service day to day for regulatory purposes, and is accountable for the quality of support young people receive.

When the manager is absent or leaves (Regulation 33)

  • If the registered service manager will be absent for 28 days or more, the provider must tell Ofsted in writing at least one month in advance, or within one week if the absence is unplanned (Regulation 33).
  • The notice must explain the reason, how long the absence will last, who will manage the service in the meantime (with their qualifications and experience), and any plans to appoint a new manager.
  • Ofsted must be told within 7 days of the manager's return.
  • If the manager leaves for good, tell Ofsted straight away, put interim arrangements in place, and apply to register a new manager as soon as possible.

What Ofsted looks for at inspection

Inspectors judge the effectiveness of leaders and managers, including management oversight, staffing, quality monitoring and the impact of supervision and training. A strong registered service manager:

  • Knows every young person's story, current risks and progress.
  • Spots patterns across the service, such as repeated missing episodes from one property, and acts on them.
  • Can show how feedback from young people has changed practice.
  • Is honest about weaknesses and has a plan to address them.
